## What the numbers say

**CRAFT is the dominant GPU cost and gains less from the T4 than expected.** 2.9
s/page on a T4 against 4.7 on the laptop's MPS: only 1.6×, because a quarter of
CRAFT's time is CPU post-processing and the instances' vCPUs are slower than the M2's
cores (their CPU-only CRAFT is 32 to 52 s/page). The L4 halves it to 1.54.

**The road UNet is where GPUs shine**: 0.53 on a T4, 0.24 on an L4, 7.4 on the best
CPU. That settles P(road) generation: GPU, no debate.

**ocr is CPU-bound and scales with workers on a real GPU.** Eight workers on the
2xlarge deliver 0.66 s/page wall (about 0.56 marginal) against 2.51 for one, so a T4
is far from saturated at eight recognizer processes; the limit is cores, at roughly
4.5 vCPU-seconds per page. Without a GPU the same work costs about 15 vCPU-seconds
per page (c6i, 8 workers, 1.94 marginal), three times more, because the CRNN forward
pass moves to the CPU. So ocr belongs on the GPU instances, and it wants every vCPU
they have.

**Per vCPU, the L4 beats the T4 by 1.8 to 2.2× on every GPU stage** at the same 4-vCPU
quota footprint. Under a vCPU quota that is the number that matters.

**CPU-only is out for the heavy stages.** CRAFT at 19 s/page and the raw sheet at 9
minutes on the best CPU box would make the corpus a multi-week job even on the full
256-vCPU Standard quota.

## Corpus budget (432,293 pages, 4,080 raw key-map sheets)

GPU-serial seconds per page, key-map sheets amortized (140 s on T4, 85 s on L4, per
sheet), ocr's GPU share taken as the 8-worker wall figure:

| | T4 | L4 |
|---|---|---|
| craft | 2.88 | 1.54 |
| road + region UNet | 0.58 | 0.27 |
| ocr GPU share | ~0.5 | ~0.4 |
| key-map sheets, amortized | 1.32 | 0.80 |
| **per page** | **~5.3** | **~3.0** |
| **corpus, GPU-hours** | **~630** | **~360** |

CPU-side work (ocr decode ~4.7 vCPU-s, fit ~5, split 0.35) is about 1,300
vCPU-hours: five hours on the 256-vCPU Standard spot quota, so fit runs on plain CPU
instances and never competes for GPU quota.

Wall time for the GPU tier under the current G-family quota (8 Spot + 8 On-Demand
vCPUs) and with an increase:

| GPU fleet | vCPUs | wall time |
|---|---|---|
| 4 × g4dn.xlarge (T4) | 16 | 6.6 days |
| 4 × g6.xlarge (L4) | 16 | 3.8 days |
| 8 × g6.xlarge | 32 | 1.9 days |
| 16 × g6.xlarge | 64 | ~1 day |

Cost is similar either way, about 250 to 350 dollars for the GPU tier (L4 spot
prices sit near on-demand, T4 spot is cheaper per hour but needs twice the hours),
plus about 30 dollars for the CPU tier.

## Decisions

1. **g6.xlarge (L4) is the instance.** Same quota footprint as the T4 xlarge, half
   the wall time, equal dollars.
2. **Split the tiers by quota, not by taste.** GPU instances run craft, the UNets,
   the key-map sheets and ocr; the Standard fleet runs split, adjacency and fit. The
   sidecar layout already separates them.
3. **Ask for 32 Spot G vCPUs** now that there is usage history, and consider
   us-east-1 as a second pool. With 8 L4s the two-day target holds.
4. **The bench found a real pipeline bug**: on x86 Linux EasyOCR quantizes the CPU
   recognizer and the fine-tuned weights could not load, so `--no-gpu` ocr had never
   worked there. Fixed in 29690ce (readers are built unquantized).

## Caveats

One volume, one run per instance type; startup-excluded figures are marginal costs,
multi-worker rows are wall time on 93 pages (8 or 16 for CPU rows) and so include the
workers' startup. Spot interruptions were not exercised (none occurred). The
laptop's CPU rows use all 8 cores for torch and are not comparable per core.
